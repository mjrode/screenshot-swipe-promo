How to Tag Photos and Screenshots on iPhone

By Michael Rode · September 21, 2026 · 4 min read · How-To

Quick answer: You can label iPhone photos with searchable captions and organize them into albums. For screenshots, a dedicated organizer can also attach reusable tags. Start with three to five categories, use consistent wording, and test a search before labeling your whole library. A hashtag in a caption is still caption text.

You save a restaurant menu, a recipe, and a delivery receipt in the same afternoon. A week later, finding any of the three means scrolling past everything else. A few consistent labels give you another way back to those images.

Can you add tags in Apple Photos on iPhone?

The practical built-in options are captions and albums. Apple documents a caption field in each photo’s information panel and supports searching those captions. You can put a label such as “recipe” there, but typing a hashtag does not turn the caption into a separate tag-management system. See Apple’s photo-information guide.

Choose the structure that matches how you look for things. An album gives you a collection to browse. A caption gives you words to search. A screenshot tag gives you a reusable category to filter inside the app where you created it.

How do you use captions as photo labels?

  1. Open a photo or screenshot in Photos, then swipe up or tap the information button.
  2. Add a short caption containing the category and the detail you will remember: “recipe chickpea curry from Maya.”
  3. Save the caption, return to Search, and look for your distinctive phrase.
  4. Repeat with two more images before committing to the naming scheme.

Use ordinary words you already say. If you search for “car repair,” a label called “automotive documentation” creates extra work. Keep one spelling for each category; alternating between “receipts,” “purchases,” and “bills” makes a small library feel scattered.

When should you use an album instead?

Use albums for collections you want to open as a group: a kitchen renovation, a trip, or a set of family photos. Apple’s album guide explains how to create and maintain them. Albums also suit people who remember an image’s appearance better than the words inside it.

A useful division is broad albums and specific descriptions. “Kitchen” can contain several appliance screenshots, while the caption on one says “dishwasher measurements for the cabinet opening.” That keeps the number of albums manageable.

Try this starter set, adapting the labels to your own captures:

  • Reference: information you expect to look up again.
  • Shopping: products you are considering or comparing.
  • Recipes: meals you want to cook.
  • Work: instructions, feedback, or project details.

A screenshot that needs a deadline should also get a reminder. A category tells you what it belongs to; a scheduled alert gives you a time to act.

What makes a tagging system hard to maintain?

Starting with twenty categories creates twenty decisions for every new image. Tagging your entire backlog before testing retrieval is another common trap. You can spend an evening organizing and still struggle to find the next receipt.

Run a small retrieval test instead. Label ten recent screenshots, close the app, then try to find three of them using only the words you remember. Merge labels that overlap and rename the ones you never think to search. The weekly organization guide shows how to keep that small system running.

What else should you know?

Can I add hashtags to iPhone photos?

You can type a hashtag into a photo caption, but it remains caption text. Treat it as a naming convention you maintain yourself. Use a distinctive word and test whether searching that word returns the image before applying the same label across a large collection.

Are tags and albums the same thing?

They serve different organizing habits. An album is a collection you open and browse. A tag is a reusable label within an app’s tagging system. Decide where you want to retrieve your screenshots, then use the labels and collections that are available in that place.

How many tags should I start with?

Three to five is a useful starting experiment. Choose categories that describe the screenshots you actually save, then add a new tag only when an existing one causes confusion. A small collection of labels is easier to remember during both filing and searching later.

Should I tag every screenshot?

Start with screenshots you expect to use again. An expired parking confirmation may need deletion, while a warranty receipt deserves a label and a short explanation. Review a small recent batch first so the time spent organizing stays proportional to the value of the saved information.

How can you organize ten screenshots today?

  1. Choose three labels that describe your recent screenshots.
  2. Apply a label and a specific note to ten keepers.
  3. Find three of those images again, then adjust any confusing labels.
